⏺ Good instinct. Here's how to handle it safely:

  Before Touching the Board

  1. Discharge static - Touch a grounded metal object (like a metal lamp base, your Mac's metal chassis while it's plugged in, or a radiator) before handling the board
  2. Avoid carpet/synthetic clothing - These build up static. If you're on carpet, touch something grounded first

  When Handling the Board

  3. Hold it by the edges - Avoid touching the metal pins, chips, or solder joints
  4. Keep it on a non-conductive surface - Wood table, the anti-static bag it came in (outside surface), or a piece of cardboard. Avoid metal surfaces or fabric
  5. Don't place it on your Mac - The metal body could short pins if the board lands wrong

  When Connecting Power

  6. Inspect the board first - Look for any obvious damage, bent pins, or loose solder blobs
  7. USB only at first - Don't connect anything else until the basic blink test works. The USB provides power and won't damage anything if plugged in correctly

  What Actually Causes Short Circuits

  - Metal objects touching exposed pins while powered
  - Dropping onto conductive surfaces while powered
  - Connecting wires incorrectly (we'll be careful when wiring the display later)

  For now, just the bare ESP32 + USB cable is very safe. The risky part comes later when we wire it to the LED panel - we'll go through that carefully.

  Go ahead and unbox it, put it on a wooden/cardboard surface, and plug in the USB-C cable.

⏺ Step 4: Wire the HUB75 Panel to the ESP32

  Before we start, a few safety notes:

  1. Unplug the ESP32 from USB before wiring anything
  2. Don't connect power to the panel yet - we'll do data wires first
  3. Double-check each connection before powering on

  Understanding the HUB75 Connector

  Your panel has a 16-pin input connector (2 rows of 8 pins). Look at the back of your panel - there should be labels or arrows indicating pin 1. The connector looks like this when viewed from the back of the panel:

          ┌─────────────────────────┐
     R1   │ 1   2 │ GND             │
     G1   │ 3   4 │ GND             │
     B1   │ 5   6 │ GND             │
    GND   │ 7   8 │ E  (not used for 32-row)
     A    │ 9  10 │ B               │
     C    │11  12 │ D               │
    CLK   │13  14 │ LAT             │
     OE   │15  16 │ GND             │
          └─────────────────────────┘

  Wiring Table

  Connect these using your jumper wires:
  ┌──────────────┬────────────────────┐
  │  HUB75 Pin   │ Wire to ESP32 GPIO │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ R1 (pin 1)   │ GPIO 25            │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ G1 (pin 3)   │ GPIO 26            │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ B1 (pin 5)   │ GPIO 27            │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ R2 (pin 2)   │ GPIO 14            │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ G2 (pin 4)   │ GPIO 12            │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ B2 (pin 6)   │ GPIO 13            │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ A (pin 9)    │ GPIO 23            │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ B (pin 10)   │ GPIO 19            │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ C (pin 11)   │ GPIO 5             │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ D (pin 12)   │ GPIO 17            │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ CLK (pin 13) │ GPIO 16            │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ LAT (pin 14) │ GPIO 4             │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ OE (pin 15)  │ GPIO 15            │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ GND (any)    │ GND on ESP32       │
  └──────────────┴────────────────────┘
  Important: Connect at least one GND wire between the panel and ESP32. This creates a common ground reference.

  Power (separate from ESP32):
  - Your panel has a separate power connector (red/black wires or screw terminals)

     ESP32 Subway Departure Display - Implementation Plan

     Summary

     Build an ESP32-based LED display that shows personalized "leave by"
     countdowns for L, G, M, and J trains. The display accounts for walk
     time to each station and shows when to leave your apartment, not when
     the train arrives.

     Hardware

     - ESP32: Standard ESP32-WROOM DevKit with USB-C
     - Display: P2.5 HUB75 64x32 LED matrix (160x80mm, 1/16 scan)
     - Power: 5V 3A USB-C adapter

     Key Features

     1. Split-screen layout: Two stations visible simultaneously (14px
     each)
     2. Auto-cycling: L+G view ↔ M/J view every 8 seconds with vertical
     slide transition
     3. Second-precision countdown: M:SS format (e.g., "5:23")
     4. "LEAVE NOW" alert: Solid amber when ≤30 seconds remaining
     5. Walk time calculation: Backend-configured per station
     6. Off hours: Display dark from 12am-6am
     7. Error handling: Warning icon when data stale >60s

     - Display single station countdown

     - Create backend/src/routes/esp32.ts
